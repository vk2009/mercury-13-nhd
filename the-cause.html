HISTORICAL ANALYSIS

Rather than a cultural uprising of gender equality, large legislative pressure combined with new technology allowed Sally Ride to embark on her STS-7 flight. A significant factor was the flight of Valentina Tereshkova of the Soviet Union, who was the first woman to go to space. Valentina “wasn’t even a pilot” and had “virtually no technical background” (28, Life Magazine 1983). The media shows the absurdity of the rules: “The U.S. could have been first to put a woman up in space simply by deciding to do so … with qualifications far more impressive than Valentina Tereshkova" (32). Along with these foreign pressures, there was internal backlash from the Mercury 13 member, Jerrie Cobb. Her testimony exposes the structural mechanism holding women back; when she notes that it is an "impossibility in this country for women to become jet test pilots," she targets the discriminatory loop (12, Washington, DC: U.S. Government Printing Office 1962). These pressures slowly forced the US to reimagine its policies and took twenty years to break these barriers, finally allowing Sally Ride to visit space.
