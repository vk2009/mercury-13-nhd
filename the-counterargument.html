The Counterargument

Was it rules or societal change?

At the same time, the Mercury 13 story unfolded against a broader women's revolution in the 1960s and 1970s, when activists pressed for equal rights, workplace access, and legal protections.

HISTORICAL ANALYSIS

Skeptics credit Ride’s historic breakthrough to the 1970’s Women’s Liberation Movement that caused a cultural shift in sexism. However, evidence suggests that the world had not matured as seamlessly, and the specific institutional changes allowed Sally Ride to fly. Pilot Wally Funk recalls NASA blocking her simply because she lacked the arbitrary “jet test pilot time” (NASA 1999). Additionally, the 1983 media discredited Ride’s contributions, remarking on whether she wore makeup during the flight, whether she cried if something went wrong, and commenting on her domestic roles, prompting her to say, “that society isn’t to the point yet where the country could just send up a woman astronaut and nobody would think twice about it” (58, Time 1983). This proves society had not fundamentally changed; rather, exclusionary rules were targeted and dismantled to allow women to participate in the space program.
