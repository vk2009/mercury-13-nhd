1978–1983  ·  The Space Shuttle & Sally Ride

The Structural Shift: Capsules to Shuttle

Mercury Era
Space Shuttle Era
Military combat-pilot instincts considered essential
Scientific and engineering credentials explicitly valued
All 7 original astronauts were military test pilots
1978 class included 6 women, 3 Black astronauts, 1 Asian-American astronaut
Jet test-pilot requirement mandatory
Jet test-pilot requirement dropped for mission specialists
June 18, 1983
Sally Ride becomes the first American woman in space aboard Space Shuttle Challenger, STS-7. She holds a Ph.D. in physics from Stanford. She is not a military jet test pilot.
HISTORICAL ANALYSIS

The immediate aftermath of these institutional changes was Sally Ride’s famous 1983 flight. Within five years of NASA restructuring its requirements, different demographics were able to travel in space, full of non-white candidates, Sally Ride, and other women (NASA 1978). This revolution allowed for female scientists, engineers, and doctors to participate in space programs, basing their benchmarks on scientific intellect and adaptability rather than pilot experience.
